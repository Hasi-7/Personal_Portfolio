I'm a first-year Engineering Science student at the University of Toronto, currently in the foundational year of one of Canada's most rigorous engineering programs. Engineering Science is designed to develop versatile, research-oriented engineers, and the breadth of the curriculum — from quantum mechanics to structural analysis to design methodology — reflects that ambition.

## What I'm Working On

Right now, I'm focused on building a strong foundation across engineering disciplines while exploring where I want to specialise. My coursework spans mathematics, physics, computer science, and engineering design, giving me a broad lens through which to approach problems.

Outside the classroom, I'm interested in projects that live at the intersection of hardware and software — embedded systems, robotics, and computational tools for engineering analysis. I believe some of the most interesting engineering challenges emerge when you have to make physical and digital systems work together.

## Beyond Engineering

When I'm not studying or working on projects, you can find me exploring new technologies, contributing to student engineering teams, or diving into whatever rabbit hole has captured my attention that week. I'm a firm believer that the best engineers are broadly curious people, and I try to let that curiosity guide me.

## Why This Portfolio

This portfolio started as an academic requirement for ESC102 (Praxis II), but I intend it to grow well beyond that. It's designed to be a living document of my engineering journey — a place where I can reflect on past work, document new projects, and track my development as a designer and engineer over time.
