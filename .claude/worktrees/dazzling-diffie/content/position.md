As an engineering student, I approach design with a deep curiosity about how things work — both the technical systems we build and the human contexts they operate within. My engineering practice is grounded in the belief that meaningful design emerges at the intersection of rigorous analysis and genuine empathy for the people a design serves.

Growing up, I was always drawn to taking things apart. Not to break them, but to understand them — to see the logic beneath the surface. That instinct has evolved into a design philosophy that values first principles thinking, iterative refinement, and honest reflection on what works and what doesn't.

I believe the best engineering designers are those who remain learners. Every project I've undertaken — from structural analysis to systems-level design — has taught me something I didn't expect to learn. The CIV102 bridge project taught me that elegance in engineering isn't about complexity; it's about understanding constraints deeply enough to find simple, robust solutions. Praxis showed me that the hardest part of design isn't generating solutions — it's framing the right problem.

My approach to engineering design is also shaped by my interest in the hardware-software boundary. I'm fascinated by systems where physical and digital components must work together seamlessly, and I believe understanding both sides makes me a more thoughtful designer.

Looking forward, I want to continue developing my ability to move fluidly between analytical and creative modes of thinking — to use frameworks and models not as rigid prescriptions, but as thinking tools that help me see problems from new angles. This portfolio represents not just what I've done, but how I think about design, and how that thinking continues to evolve.
