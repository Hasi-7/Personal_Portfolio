explanation: "Pairwise comparison is a prioritisation technique that evaluates items (requirements, criteria, or concepts) by comparing them two at a time. For each pair, you decide which is more important, and the cumulative results produce a ranked list. This avoids the difficulty of ranking many items simultaneously and surfaces relative priorities that may not be obvious from direct ranking."
evidence: "In the CIV102 bridge project, I used pairwise comparison to prioritise our design requirements — load capacity vs. weight vs. cost vs. constructability vs. aesthetics. The exercise revealed that our team had different implicit priorities, and the pairwise process surfaced and resolved these differences before they became design conflicts."
utilityAssessment: "Useful for prioritisation tasks where direct ranking feels arbitrary. The method forces explicit trade-off decisions and produces a defensible ranking. However, it can become unwieldy with many items and assumes transitivity, which doesn't always hold in practice."
fitAssessment: "Good fit for specific use cases. I find it most valuable when prioritising requirements or criteria at the start of a project. It's a useful complement to other convergence tools rather than a standalone method."
