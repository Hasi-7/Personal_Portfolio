explanation: "Analogical thinking involves drawing parallels between the current design challenge and solutions from different domains, contexts, or scales. By asking 'what else works like this?' or 'how has nature or another industry solved a similar problem?', designers can import proven principles and avoid reinventing solutions that already exist."
evidence: "In the CIV102 bridge project, I studied real-world bridge designs — particularly Warren and Pratt truss configurations — to understand the structural principles behind their geometry. By analysing why certain configurations distribute loads more efficiently, I was able to make informed decisions about our model bridge design rather than relying on trial and error."
utilityAssessment: "Very useful when applied thoughtfully. The key is identifying the right level of abstraction for the analogy — copying surface features of a solution rarely works, but importing the underlying principle often does. The technique is most powerful when you look beyond your own domain."
fitAssessment: "Strong fit with my thinking style. I naturally look for patterns and connections across domains, and analogical thinking formalises that instinct into a deliberate design tool. I find it particularly valuable in the early divergent phase when the solution space is still wide open."
