explanation: "A requirements list is a structured document that captures the specific, measurable criteria a design must satisfy. Requirements are typically categorised as functional (what the design must do), non-functional (qualities it must have), and constraints (boundaries it must respect). Well-written requirements are testable, traceable to stakeholder needs, and prioritised."
evidence: "In Praxis II, our team used a weighted requirements list linked to stakeholder needs and tradeoffs. We used it to compare concept directions, justify convergence decisions, and evaluate prototype iterations against consistent criteria rather than ad-hoc preferences."
utilityAssessment: "This was one of the most useful convergence tools because it connected stakeholder framing to measurable decision-making. Once criteria and weightings were explicit, team discussions became faster and more defensible."
fitAssessment: "This is a strong fit with my design practice because I work best when broad stakeholder concerns are converted into clear, testable criteria. It helps me stay rigorous while still accounting for real tradeoffs."
