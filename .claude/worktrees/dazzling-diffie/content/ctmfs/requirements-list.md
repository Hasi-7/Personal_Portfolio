explanation: "A requirements list is a structured document that captures the specific, measurable criteria a design must satisfy. Requirements are typically categorised as functional (what the design must do), non-functional (qualities it must have), and constraints (boundaries it must respect). Well-written requirements are testable, traceable to stakeholder needs, and prioritised."
evidence: "For CIV102, I developed a requirements list that translated project specifications into engineering requirements with clear metrics. In Praxis II, our requirements list was more complex, incorporating both quantitative and qualitative requirements with traceability back to specific stakeholder needs."
utilityAssessment: "Fundamental to effective convergence. A clear requirements list transforms subjective evaluation into objective assessment. The process of writing requirements also reveals gaps in your understanding — if you can't specify a measurable requirement, you probably don't understand the need well enough."
fitAssessment: "Core tool in my practice. I've learned that investing time in a thorough requirements list pays dividends throughout the design process. It serves as both a guide for concept generation and a rubric for evaluation, creating coherence between the divergent and convergent phases."
