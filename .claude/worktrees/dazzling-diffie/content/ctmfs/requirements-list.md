explanation: "A requirements list is a structured document that captures the specific, measurable criteria a design must satisfy. Requirements are typically categorised as functional (what the design must do), non-functional (qualities it must have), and constraints (boundaries it must respect). Well-written requirements are testable, traceable to stakeholder needs, and prioritised [2], [14]."
evidence: "In Praxis II, our team used a weighted requirements list linked to stakeholder needs and tradeoffs. We used it to compare concept directions, justify convergence decisions, and evaluate prototype iterations against consistent criteria rather than ad-hoc preferences [10], [14]."
utilityAssessment: "This was one of the most useful convergence tools because it connected stakeholder framing to measurable decision-making. Once criteria and weightings were explicit, team discussions became faster and more defensible [10], [15]."
fitAssessment: "This fits my position because it turns broad concerns into testable criteria and keeps scope credible. Explicit requirements helped avoid over-engineering by focusing only on what could be verified and delivered within the project's constraints [2], [10], [15]."
