explanation: "A Pugh Chart (or Pugh Matrix) is a systematic concept evaluation tool that compares design alternatives against a set of criteria relative to a reference concept. Each concept is scored as better (+), same (S), or worse (−) than the reference on each criterion. The method reveals which concepts are broadly strong and highlights specific trade-offs."
evidence: "In Praxis I, we used a Pugh Chart to evaluate our top 6 concepts against criteria derived from our stakeholder research. In Praxis II, we added weighting to reflect stakeholder priorities, making the evaluation more nuanced and producing a more defensible selection."
utilityAssessment: "One of the most reliable convergence tools I've used. Its strength is in making comparison explicit and systematic rather than intuitive. The forced comparison against a reference prevents the common trap of evaluating concepts in isolation. The main limitation is that it can oversimplify complex trade-offs into +/S/− categories."
fitAssessment: "Excellent fit with my practice. As someone who values structured analysis, the Pugh Chart appeals to my preference for evidence-based decision-making. I've found it particularly valuable in team settings because it makes the reasoning behind a selection transparent and debatable."
