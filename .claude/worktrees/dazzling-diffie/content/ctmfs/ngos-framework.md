explanation: "The NGOs Framework (Needs, Goals, Objectives) is a framing tool that translates a broad problem into three levels: what users actually need, what the design should achieve, and how success will be measured. It helps prevent jumping to solutions by keeping design decisions tied to stakeholder outcomes [1], [5]."
evidence: "In Praxis I, we used NGOs while rescoping the adopted whiteboard brief. We reframed the core need as preventing smudging and ink transfer for left-handed writers, set goals around preserving natural writing and comfort, and defined measurable objectives for writing-speed retention, low visible smudging, ergonomic wrist posture, and acceptable mass [5], [6]."
utilityAssessment: "NGOs made convergence more rigorous because every concept could be tested against the same objective criteria instead of preference. It also helped eliminate concepts that performed well in one area but failed the broader design intent [6]."
fitAssessment: "This fits how I approach engineering design because it lets me break a problem into core functionalities and tackle each one individually. Once each part is defined and working on its own, I can focus on integration and isolate issues more clearly when they appear [5], [6]."
