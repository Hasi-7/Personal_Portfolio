evidence: |
  In CIV102, we used structural calculations based on loading and section behavior to estimate critical regions and expected failure tendencies before real life testing. These model-based predictions informed our design choices and where to reinforce the bridge [8]. Figure 1 shows the modelling output we used to compare load effects and decide where reinforcement was necessary.

utilityAssessment: "Mathematical modelling improved confidence in our design decisions by providing quantitative guidance before construction. It narrowed the solution space and reduced purely trial-and-error adjustments [8], [9]. However, it is not perfect and can sometimes fail to account for a failure mode. For example, our bridge failed due to the splice being weaker than expected and the mathematical model we created did not account for it."
fitAssessment: "This fits my position because I work by breaking complex systems into manageable parts, testing assumptions, and adjusting from evidence. Mathematical modelling supports that directly: it lets me isolate one variable at a time, check expected behavior before building, and only commit once the numbers and construction implications are both credible [8], [9]."
