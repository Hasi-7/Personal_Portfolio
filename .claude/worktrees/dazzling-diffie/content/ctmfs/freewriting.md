explanation: "Freewriting is my primary diverging tool for generating a large quantity of concepts without filtering too early. I use it to force ideation without the limitation of implementation, make hidden assumptions obvious, and capture partial thoughts that can later be refined into testable concepts [1], [7]."
evidence: |
  In Praxis I, our freewriting rounds produced many concepts before convergence, including quicka new quick-drying ink, a dummy-marker, air-cushion or fan-assisted support, marker-mounted platforms, and rolling support mechanisms. As shown in Figure 6, the freewriting output contains a mix of feasible and exploratory ideas, which is what made it valuable for early divergence. Across our diverging methods, freewriting consistently generated ideas that were most reusable in later concept development [6], [7].

  ![Praxis I freewriting evidence](/images/projects/praxis-i/figure-6.png)

utilityAssessment: "Freewriting was most useful for breaking the initial cognitive anchor and giving our team enough concept diversity to compare meaningfully during convergence. It also exposed early technical questions and pointed us toward reference designs faster than discussion-only ideation [6], [7]. Its main limitation is that raw output quality varies, so it must be followed by a round of filtering through discussion and secondary research rather than treated as decision evidence on its own."
fitAssessment: "This fits my position of widening the space first, then letting evidence drive the next decision. Strong concepts emerge when time and effort is given to diverging, but freewriting still needs to be filtered with convergence tools so quantity is not prioritized above quality [6], [7]."
