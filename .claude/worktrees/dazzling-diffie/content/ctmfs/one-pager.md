explanation: "A one-pager is a concise, visually structured summary of a design project that communicates the key information — problem, approach, solution, and evidence — on a single page. The constraint of one page forces clarity and prioritisation: every element must earn its space."
evidence: "In both Praxis I and Praxis II, I created one-page project summaries as final deliverables. These documents synthesised weeks of work into a clear narrative supported by visuals, key data, and concise text. The Praxis II one-pager was significantly more refined — I had learned from Praxis I that visual hierarchy and information flow matter as much as the content itself."
utilityAssessment: "Highly useful as both a communication tool and a thinking tool. The act of creating a one-pager forces you to identify what actually matters about your project and articulate it clearly. It's also a practical skill — in professional engineering, the ability to communicate complex work concisely is as important as the technical work itself."
fitAssessment: "Strong fit, and an area of significant growth. My early one-pagers were dense text blocks with token visuals. I've learned to think of them as designed artefacts — balancing text, visuals, and white space to create something that communicates effectively at a glance while rewarding deeper reading."
