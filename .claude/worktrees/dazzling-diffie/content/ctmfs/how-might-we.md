explanation: "\"How Might We\" (HMW) is a reframing technique that converts identified problems or insights into actionable design opportunities. By phrasing challenges as open-ended questions beginning with \"How might we...\", designers shift from problem-dwelling to opportunity-seeking while keeping scope appropriately bounded."
evidence: "During Praxis I, after completing our stakeholder research, we generated 15+ HMW statements to reframe our findings into design opportunities. We voted on and clustered these to identify the most promising directions. One HMW statement transformed a complaint about paperwork into 'How might we capture intake information in a way that feels natural and doesn't interrupt the conversation?'"
utilityAssessment: "Extremely useful as a bridge between research and ideation. The technique's strength is in its constraint: it forces you to be specific enough to be actionable but open enough to allow creative solutions. The biggest risk is writing HMW statements that are too broad or too narrow."
fitAssessment: "Good fit with my practice. I appreciate how HMW imposes discipline on the transition from understanding to ideation — a transition that can otherwise feel arbitrary. I've found it particularly useful when working in teams, as it creates a shared, tangible artefact everyone can rally around."
