explanation: "Concept sketching involves creating quick, low-fidelity visual representations of design ideas to communicate and explore concepts rapidly. The emphasis is on speed and communication over artistic quality — the goal is to make ideas tangible enough to discuss, compare, and iterate on without investing significant time in any single concept."
evidence: "During Praxis I, I used concept sketching extensively during the divergent phase, creating annotated sketches that communicated both the physical form and user interaction flow of each concept. These sketches became the primary artefacts we used in our Pugh Chart evaluation, making abstract ideas concrete enough to compare meaningfully."
utilityAssessment: "Highly useful for making the leap from verbal descriptions to something tangible. Teams often think they agree on an idea until someone sketches it — the visual representation surfaces assumptions and disagreements that words alone can hide. It's also an efficient way to generate variants of a concept quickly."
fitAssessment: "Good fit, though I initially undervalued it. As someone more comfortable with analytical tools, I used to view sketching as 'not real engineering.' Praxis I changed that perspective — I saw firsthand how a simple sketch could resolve a 20-minute team debate in 30 seconds."
