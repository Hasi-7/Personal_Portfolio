explanation: |
  Iterative design is a cycle of proposing, evaluating, and refining design options over multiple passes. Instead of committing to one concept immediately, the team improves the design through repeated adjustments informed by feedback and analysis [3], [8].

  ![Cross-section of modified second iteration](/images/projects/civ102-bridge/figure-4.png)
  *Figure 1: Cross-section of the second iteration, modified to maximize crucial properties, such as the value of the second moment of area and the neutral axis. Design 0 Modified solidified the elimination of a design akin to Design 0 due to its vulnerability to compressive forces [8].*

  ![Split I-beam inspired iteration](/images/projects/civ102-bridge/figure-5.png)
  *Figure 2: Takes inspiration from a traditional I-Beam but splits it at the leg, however resulted in too much wasted material for not enough strength, thus discarding this cross section [8].*

evidence: "In CIV102, our bridge design evolved through repeated revisions to geometry and member layout as we compared alternatives and checked expected performance. We used each iteration to identify governing failure modes and decide what to change next, instead of treating the first concept as fixed [8], [9]."

utilityAssessment: "Its main utility was reducing early design attachment. Iteration allowed us to improve weaker parts of the bridge concept before finalizing construction details [8], [9]. It allowed us to identify what aspects of a current iteration are strongest while being able to differentiate the weaknesses, thus giving a direction to move forward with."
fitAssessment: "This fits my position because I work by breaking complex systems into manageable parts, testing behavior, and then adjusting based on what the evidence shows. Iterative design mirrors that exact approach: each bridge revision became a focused test of one assumption, then informed the next change. It also keeps me from becoming attached to an idea when there are many other things to be verified, such as constructability [8], [9] [4]."
