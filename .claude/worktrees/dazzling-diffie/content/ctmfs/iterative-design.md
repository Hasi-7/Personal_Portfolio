explanation: "Iterative design is a cycle of proposing, evaluating, and refining design options over multiple passes. Instead of committing to one concept immediately, the team improves the design through repeated adjustments informed by feedback and analysis."
evidence: |
  In CIV102, our bridge design evolved through repeated revisions to geometry and member layout as we compared alternatives and checked expected performance. We used each iteration to identify governing failure modes and decide what to change next, instead of treating the first concept as fixed.

  ![Cross-section of modified second iteration](/images/projects/civ102-bridge/figure-4.png)
  *Figure 1: Cross-section of the second iteration, modified to maximize crucial properties, such as the value of the second moment of area and the neutral axis. Design 0 Modified solidified the elimination of a design akin to Design 0 due to its vulnerability to compressive forces.*

  ![Split I-beam inspired iteration](/images/projects/civ102-bridge/figure-5.png)
  *Figure 2: Takes inspiration from a traditional IBeam but splits it at the leg, however resulted in too much wasted material for not enough strength, thus discarding this cross section.*
utilityAssessment: "Its main utility was reducing early design lock-in. Iteration allowed us to improve weaker parts of the bridge concept before finalizing construction details."
fitAssessment: "This is a strong fit for me because I rarely get the best design in one pass. Iterative design supports how I work: test assumptions, improve the concept, and repeat until tradeoffs are better balanced."
