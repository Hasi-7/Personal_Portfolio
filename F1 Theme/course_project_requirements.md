# Course Project Requirements
## ESC102 — Engineering Science Praxis II
## Student Engineer Design Portfolio

---

## Course: ESC102 Engineering Science Praxis II
## Weight: 15% of final grade
## Submitter: Individual
## Due: As per exams schedule

---

## What the Portfolio Is

A hybrid of:
1. A **professional design portfolio** — showcases products of design work
2. An **engineering handbook** — personal reference of CTMFs for future use

It must provide evidence that the student meets the three high-level course learning outcomes:
1. Engage in quality, credible engineering design
2. Express engineering designs using modes appropriate to engineering
3. Work effectively as a member of a team

---

## Three Required Components

### Component 1: Position in Context
- A statement of position as an engineering designer
- Must **iterate on the original Position Statement** from January 2026 (not just repeat it)
- Must reflect how the position has evolved through the semester
- Should establish the basis for how the portfolio is organized
- Should frame the selection of CTMFs discussed

### Component 2: Design Products
- Summaries of design experiences
- Must give enough context that someone unfamiliar with the project can understand what was done and why
- Must emphasize how the student's position was enacted in the designs
- Must balance documentation of process AND product

### Component 3: CTMFs (Concepts, Tools, Models, Frameworks)
- Provide evidence of use spanning the entire Frame → Diverge → Converge → Represent process
- Evaluate the effectiveness of each CTMF
- Assessment should address: when to use it, when not to, and what can be gained

## Hard Requirements (SHALL)

These are mandatory. Violations result in automatic mark deductions (-0.5 per violation after first).

1. **Shall be submitted to Quercus as a single PDF document.**
   - If website: PDF must contain the website URL AND screenshots of all main pages taken before deadline.

2. **Shall include a reference list** in Praxis embedded extract format (APA or IEEE).
   - Website submissions: references on website; source extracts in the PDF submitted to Quercus.

3. **Shall include a position statement** — an iteration on the January position statement, reflecting growth.

4. **Shall include one-page summaries of these three primary design activities** (completed since September 2025):
   - Praxis I project
   - CIV102 Bridge Project
   - Praxis II project

5. **For each design project, shall include an annotation** that:
   - Explains and interprets the project
   - Describes the design process in context
   - Links the process to the student's position

6. **Shall only include design projects done since September 2025.**
   (Exception: prior experience may appear in "position in context" section)

7. **For each of the three required projects, shall include at least 3 distinct CTMFs** used in or relevant to that project.
   - Total minimum: **9 distinct CTMFs**
   - A CTMF may connect to more than one project
   - Must still have at least 3 per project

8. **Shall include CTMFs covering ALL four strands of Frame → Diverge → Converge → Represent.**
   - FRAME: tools used to understand and define the design problem
   - DIVERGE: tools used to generate ideas and explore possibilities
   - CONVERGE: tools used to evaluate, select, and refine ideas
   - REPRESENT: tools used to communicate, prototype, or present designs

9. **Should include only CTMFs for which evidence of use can be provided** (or credibly connected retroactively).

10. **Shall give credit for design work performed by teammates** per:
    - University of Toronto Code of Academic Behaviour
    - Professional Engineers Ontario Code of Ethics

## Evaluation Criteria

Graders evaluate on these dimensions (Outstanding is the goal):

### 1. Clarity of Position in Context
- **Outstanding**: Position on engineering design is woven into all design products in a critical, reflective way that balances positive and negative implications.
- *Grader thought: "I have a really good understanding of how you approach engineering design and why."*

### 2. Validity of Position (Evidence)
- **Outstanding**: Projects are integrated and contrasted to show nuances of position applied to engineering design.
- *Grader thought: "I can see how your position is playing into how you see engineering design from how this is set up."*

### 3. Appropriateness of Evidence Selection
- **Outstanding**: Discussion demonstrates development and reflection on values, practice, and engineering identity.
- *Grader thought: "For each project, I feel like I have just the right amount of information."*

### 4. Utility of Summaries and Annotations
- **Outstanding**: Annotations highlight skills and experiences gained; position is woven into presentations critically and reflectively.
- *Grader thought: "Your annotations help me gain a deeper understanding of your process."*

### 5. Appropriateness of CTMF Selection
- **Outstanding**: CTMFs show a broad range from across design stages; assessments are both personal and transferable.
- *Grader thought: "This set of CTMFs shows you approached all strands of the design process with rigour."*

### 6. Utility of CTMF Explanations
- **Outstanding**: Annotations focus attention on specific features; explanations show where values and biases influenced design.
- *Grader thought: "It's easy for me to understand why you've included this CTMF."*

### 7. Ease of Navigation
- **Outstanding**: Unique and meaningful arrangement, easy to follow, clearly demonstrates how approach supports future work.
- *Grader thought: "This portfolio is easy to follow and organized in a logical and relevant way."*

### 8. Clarity of Written Prose
- **Outstanding**: Writing demonstrates unique voice, reveals engineering identity through word choice and structure.
- *Grader thought: "The writing allowed me to understand your process efficiently as uniquely yours."*

### 9. Quality of Visual Elements
- **Outstanding**: Layout and visuals enhance comprehension; connections between visuals and explanations enhance understanding.
- *Grader thought: "I can quickly grasp key ideas from your visuals and I'm never unsure where to focus."*
