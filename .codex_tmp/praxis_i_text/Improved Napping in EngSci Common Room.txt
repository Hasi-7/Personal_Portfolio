Introduction

This design brief will explore the current inadequacies faced when napping on the University of
Toronto’s Engineering Science (EngSci) common room chairs; it will then outline objectives for
a more ergonomic, comfortable and portable solution that can be integrated with the chairs.
Currently, students complain of back and neck pain after napping in these chairs [1], indicating a
lack of comfort and proper ergonomics for taking a nap. Over time, this can result in lasting
detriments to the health of students [2]. Thus, access to an ergonomic napping solution in the
common room is vital to improve the energy level of sleep deprived EngSci students without
causing long-term health detriments.


Background
   1.​ Napping Definition
         a.​ Napping will be defined as a light and brief sleep that lasts less than 30 minutes
             [1][3]. Body positions for napping can be more varied than those for sleeping
             because of the shorter time frame, so designs are not required to act as a
             traditional mattress or offer entirely horizontal support for the body.

   2.​ Environment Definition and Reasoning
          a.​ The working environment of the design will be the
              chairs in the EngSci common room, not including
              high chairs or couches. Figure 1 shows an example
              chair in the EngSci common room for the design.
              Designs should not seek to replace the chair, but
              integrate with it.
          b.​ The environment has been limited to the EngSci
              common room chairs because entry requires an
              EngSci student’s T-Card. This means that safety
              during the nap is less of a concern for the design.
          c.​ Since it is not socially acceptable to sleep on the
              floor, lie down across a couch or on a table to sleep;
              designs should not rely on these surfaces.​

Stakeholder Expectations
This section will discuss all relevant stakeholders for the design of a napping device, and list
what they want in a design.

   1.​ UofT Engineering Science Students
       EngSci students are the primary stakeholders because they are the ones napping in the
       common room [1]. They want a design that can integrate with the existing chairs to make
       them more ergonomic and comfortable, without replacing the original chairs. Students
       also want a solution that is portable enough to be carried around campus, because they
       must travel between classes and home. Finally, they want a design that requires minimal
       cleaning effort to stay hygienic.
   2.​ People within the vicinity of the product
       These secondary stakeholders want designs to take up minimal space in the common
       room.
   3.​ Design Team


Need, Goals, and Objectives
Primary Stakeholder Need
UofT Engineering Science students need an attachment to Engineering Science Common Room
chairs to make napping more comfortable and ergonomic, while also being portable and
hygienic.

To satisfy this need, four goals with their associated objectives will be identified and justified.

   1.​ Provides an ergonomic resting place
       The current common room chairs are not ideal for napping due to their structure having
       backrests of angles 80o-90o. Thus, the design will allow EngSci students to sleep with
       their backs/necks straight instead of hunched over, preventing chronic back and neck
       problems in the long term.

Reference Designs
For each reference design, features that satisfy objectives will be discussed, as well as objectives
not met. From this analysis the reader should gain an idea of what features may be useful to
implement in a solution.

Portable Head Rest

One existing design is a portable head rest as shown in Figure
2. It consists of a supporting board that is inserted into a
traveller’s bag, with a hinge attached to the cushion.

It satisfies Objective 3.1 - 3.3 because it is within the portable
dimensions (21.59cm×15.24cm×2.22cm <
43cm×25cm×15cm) [24]. The design takes advantage of
features that the environment has, such as the chair and the
user’s bag, to support the user’s body weight, so that it
doesn’t have to waste space on providing this support.

However, this design does not satisfy Goal 1-2 and Goal 4.

Conclusion
Considering that unergonomic naps are so common in the EngSci common room, hopefully
solutions inspired by this design brief can improve the ergonomics and comfort of those naps,
while providing a portable and hygienic product at the same time. These goals and objectives
were created after an analysis of EngSci student expectations to ensure that primary stakeholder
wants were represented as best as possible. Therefore, any design under the outlined brief should
transform ordinary office chairs in the EngSci common room into ergonomic napping
environments, improving student rest quality and productivity.

A.2 Skeletal Muscle Definition
​       To clarify objective 2.3, “skeletal
muscles” include every skeletal muscle that is
entirely voluntarily controlled, such as the
shoulder, hamstring, or abdominal muscles
[26] [27]. This does not include cardiac or
smooth muscle, as those are not considered
skeletal muscles [26]. Cardiac and smooth
muscle are essential for functions like
pumping the heart or breathing—which must
take place during sleep—so these are not
included in the muscles that should be at rest (otherwise the user would have to be dead for the
design to satisfy objective 2.3!). The diaphragm and extraocular muscles are excluded from
muscles that should be at rest because these are active even during REM sleep [28].
